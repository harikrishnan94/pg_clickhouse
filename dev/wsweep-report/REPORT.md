# W-sweep + producer-phase-split — RESULTS

Optimally-tuned native PostgreSQL vs SHM-offload wall time, swept over W={1,2,4,8} cores under a
shared cgroup-v2 cpu.max cap (both the PG postmaster tree AND the live ClickHouse server in ONE
cgroup). Warm median of N=5 runs/cell. For every offloaded cell, the producer is decomposed into
READ / DEFORM / PUBLISH cores (+PUBLISH_STALL ms) plus the ClickHouse CONSUMER. See
00-PRE-REGISTRATION.md for the frozen protocol and 90-CONVERGENCE for the G1-G4 evidence.

Speedup = native_median / offload_median (>1 => offload faster). Producer and consumer run
CONCURRENTLY (streaming pipeline): cores are additive, wall-times are NOT.

## Executive summary

Native PostgreSQL 18 (optimally tuned, JIT, work_mem 2GB, no spill) vs pg_clickhouse SHM-offload,
swept over **W ∈ {1,2,4,8} cores** under a **shared cgroup-v2 `cpu.max` cap holding BOTH the PG
postmaster tree AND the live ClickHouse server** (so the two engines compete for the *same* W cores).
Warm median of **N=5** runs/cell. Every offloaded cell passes three oracles (offload / liveness /
correctness); every offloaded cell additionally reports the producer decomposed into **READ / DEFORM /
PUBLISH** cores (+PUBLISH_STALL) and the ClickHouse **CONSUMER**, validated by **two independent
instruments that converge (G1–G4)**.

Headline (over ALL eligible queries, losses included — no cherry-picking):

| benchmark | W | queries | faster | geomean | median | min | max |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| ClickBench | 2 | 42 | 41 | **2.03×** | 1.81× | 0.66× | 6.73× |
| ClickBench | 4 | 42 | 41 | **2.14×** | 1.82× | 0.67× | 9.56× |
| ClickBench | 8 | 42 | 41 | **2.59×** | 2.00× | 0.72× | **17.48×** |
| ClickBench | 1 | 42 | 23 | 1.24× | 1.11× | 0.36× | 4.08× |

| TPC-H | 1 | 11 | 6 | 0.85× | 1.05× | 0.14× | 3.84× |

- **ClickBench**: offload wins broadly and grows with W — at W≥2, **41/42 queries faster**, geomean
  rising 2.03× → 2.59× from W=2 → W=8, max 17.48× (Q33). Only one query (Q43) is ≤1× at W≥2. Even at
  W=1 (where one core is shared by producer **and** consumer) it wins on 23/42.
- **TPC-H** is mixed and query-shape-dependent: offload wins big on join/aggregate-heavy queries
  (**Q14 6.83×, Q9 3.24×, Q1 2.90×, Q3 1.99×, Q7 1.69×** at W=8) and loses where the offload must
  stream a large relation that native prunes/aggregates more cheaply itself: Q19 0.10× (offload streams
  all 60M lineitem rows while native's selective part-OR join touches far fewer), Q6 0.40× (trivial
  filter+sum — nothing for the consumer to accelerate), Q4 0.47× (CH's streamed semi-join is slower than
  PG's hash semi-join here). The W=8 median sits at parity (0.98×) because the candidate set mixes both.
- **Excluded (root-caused, matches pre-registration)**: TPC-H **Q8** offloads and is live but its
  `mkt_share = sum(Decimal)/sum(Decimal)` division loses scale in ClickHouse (~5e-4 rel, D0008) —
  beyond the allowed fidelity bounds → correctness FAIL; TPC-H **Q11** the offload oracle does not fire
  (the non-correlated HAVING subquery becomes an unbound PARAM_EXEC, D0008). ClickBench: **zero**
  excluded — all 42 correct within the documented bounds.

Protocol frozen *before* running in `00-PRE-REGISTRATION.md`; full environment, SHAs, GUCs, cgroup setup
and exact commands in `10-REPRODUCTION.md`.

## Correctness oracle — verdict accounting

All offloaded results were compared to native within the documented bounded deviations only
(F1 decimal display-scale; F3 avg→Float64 ≤~1e-15 rel; F5 bpchar trailing blanks; top-N tie reshuffle
under a deterministic tiebreak). Verdict counts across all cells:

- ClickBench (168 cells): 108 `exact`, 8 `approx`(≤1e-15), 16 `topN` (set-identical, order-only),
  8 `empty-both` (both engines return 0 rows — agree), 28 `DIFF` → **all 7 underlying top-N queries
  (Q18,22,32,33,39,40,41) adjudicated BENIGN** by `dev/clickbench/tiebreak_check.sh`: each collapses to
  `exact` once a deterministic total order is imposed, proving the difference was equal-ranked rows at
  the LIMIT boundary, not a value error (evidence: `evidence/topn-tiebreak-adjudication.txt`). A real
  value bug would stay DIFF under the total order.
- TPC-H (44 eligible cells over 11 queries): `exact`/`approx`/`topN` throughout; the **only** real DIFF
  is Q8 (excluded, above).

## Convergence gates G1–G4 (the split is invalid unless independent instruments agree)

Two independent instruments measure the producer phase split, plus two OS/DB cross-checks. Noise band
defined (pre-registration) as `~= := relative diff ≤ max(5%, 1 stdev)`.

- **G1 — in-code self-consistency (PASS, <0.02%).** Per-phase `CLOCK_THREAD_CPUTIME_ID` summed
  (READ+DEFORM+PUBLISH+STALL) vs the worker's independent `getrusage(RUSAGE_SELF)` CPU. On the Q6 proof
  cell, all 4 workers: |sum − getrusage| ≤ 0.2 ms (<0.02%). The stopwatch partitions the timeline, so
  this holds by construction and confirms the timers are wired correctly.
- **G2 — in-code timer vs perf (PASS).** perf PMU-`cycles`, 160,707 samples, DWARF-inline full-stack
  classification (51,690 producer samples): **READ 73.2% / DEFORM 25.3% / PUBLISH 1.5%** vs in-code
  **70.3 / 28.5 / 1.2** — READ within 4.1% rel, PUBLISH within 0.3 pt, DEFORM within ~1 stdev. Two
  instruments at different layers (thread CPU clock vs hardware PMU) agree. Evidence:
  `evidence/G1-G2-convergence-q6.txt`.
- **G3 — in-code reader cores vs /proc off_prod (PASS w/ explained residual).** The reader phases
  account for ~82–90% of the OS-measured off_prod; the residual (~0.4–1.5 cores) is **non-reader
  producer-side PG CPU**: PG18 `io_method=worker` io-workers performing the heap reads, the coordinating
  backend's deparse/dispatch, and stream-worker lifecycle (spawn/columnizer-init/EOS/socket-pump). The
  residual is consistent and named, not noise. The *reported* read/deform/publish cores are off_prod ×
  the (G2-validated) in-code CPU fractions, so they sum to off_prod by construction.
- **G4 — /proc off_cons vs CH query_log consumer CPU (PASS w/ explained gap).** Per-cell, off_cons
  (CH process CPU/wall) vs CH `query_log` (User+SystemTimeMicroseconds / query_duration). They agree
  closely on most cells (e.g. TPC-H Q1 1.29 vs 1.28; Q14 0.35 vs 0.32; many ClickBench within 0.02);
  the gaps are CH background CPU inside the longer /proc wall window (off_cons averages over the whole
  psql wall, the query_log figure is query-attributed only). See the per-cell appendix table below.

## What the split shows (each claim backed by two converging sources)

**1. The SHM producer is READ-dominated; PUBLISH is negligible.**
The heap page scan + tuple visibility classify (READ) is the largest producer phase across the board.
- Source A (in-code timer): READ = 70.3% of producer CPU on the Q6 proof cell; READ is the dominant
  phase in 10/11 TPC-H queries and 41/42 ClickBench queries (per-query split tables above).
- Source B (perf PMU, independent): READ = 73.2% of producer on-CPU samples (G2).
- Corollary, both sources: PUBLISH (the memcpy of built columns into the SHM ring slot) is **~1–2%**
  (in-code 1.2%, perf 1.5%). This independently re-confirms that the staging→ring copy is not a
  bottleneck — a "direct-to-ring" producer rewrite would buy essentially nothing.

**2. PUBLISH_STALL directly exposes which side paces the streaming pipeline.**
The stall is the producer's ring-full backpressure wait — time it idles because the consumer hasn't
drained a slot. It is ~0 when the producer is the bottleneck and large when the consumer is.
- Source A (in-code stall timer): near-zero on Q6/Q14 (consumer keeps up); large on TPC-H Q3
  (10–20 s summed across producers) and Q4/Q5 (several seconds) — there the producers block on the ring.
- Source B (consumer cores): exactly those high-stall cells carry the largest off_cons / consumer_ms
  (Q3 off_cons rises 0.57→3.66 across W; Q9 off_cons 5.27 > off_prod 2.69), i.e. the consumer is doing
  the heavy work and pacing the pipeline. On such cells the producer's core figure UNDERSTATES its
  throughput — it is throttled to consumer speed, not scan-bound.

**3. TPC-H offload wins iff ClickHouse's engine removes more work from PG than the producer scan adds.**
- Q14 (6.83×) and Q1 (2.90×): producer-bound in cores (off_prod ≫ off_cons) yet big winners — native
  must run the join/4×sum+3×avg itself, while CH does it for ~0.3–1.3 consumer cores. Source A: off_cons
  tiny in /proc; Source B: CH `query_log` consumer time tiny (G4) vs the multi-second native wall.
- Q9 (3.24×): the one **core-consumer-bound** TPC-H cell (off_cons 5.27 > off_prod 2.69) — the 6-way
  join+GROUP BY is genuinely heavy and CH's vectorized, multi-threaded engine beats PG's gather. Source
  A: /proc off_cons > off_prod; Source B: G4 query_log consumer cores 4.92 corroborates off_cons 5.27.
- Losers: Q6 (0.40×) — trivial sum, off_cons ~0.36, offload only adds deform+publish over a scan native
  does for free; Q19 (0.10×) — offload streams the full 60M-row lineitem (off_prod 4.08, DEFORM-dominant)
  while native's selective part-OR join prunes most of it; Q4 (0.47×) — high stall + off_cons 1.6 show
  CH's streamed semi-join is the slower engine for that shape. Source A: speedup<1 (clean headline);
  Source B: the split (off_prod/off_cons/stall) explains the mechanism in each case.

**4. ClickBench wins broadly because offload parallelizes the aggregate that native must gather through
one backend.** 41/42 producer-bound by cores (off_prod ~7.3, off_cons ~0.1–0.9), READ+DEFORM-heavy
(wide 105-column `hits` rows). Yet offload beats native 2.0–2.6× median at W≥2 and up to 17.5× (Q33).
- Source A (aggregate distribution): faster-count 41/42 and geomean rising 2.03→2.59× as W grows — the
  CH consumer scales the GROUP BY with max_threads while native is Gather/single-backend-bound.
- Source B (per-query split): the biggest wins are the highest-cardinality GROUP BYs (Q33 17.5×, Q32
  3.4×) where CH returns k=10 rows after a parallel aggregate, whereas native materializes/sorts the
  full grouped set in one backend. This matches the previously documented single-PG-backend consumer
  ceiling that the offload removes.

**5. W=1 is reported honestly and liveness holds.** At W=1 the single core is shared producer≈consumer
(Q1: off_prod 0.52 + off_cons 0.50 ≈ 1.0 core). Offload still wins where the native single-core operation
is pathological (Q1 3.84×, ClickBench 23/42 faster) and loses where producer overhead cannot be hidden
under one core. Crucially, no W=1 cell deadlocked.
- Source A (liveness oracle): CH `read_rows` advanced to the full relation on every W=1 cell
  (lineitem 59,986,052; hits 10,000,000) with a clean QueryFinish — no frozen read_rows.
- Source B (cores sum): off_prod + off_cons ≈ 1.0 at W=1 confirms both stages ran on the one capped core.

## G3/G4 residual notes (so the cross-checks are not over-claimed)
- G3 residual = off_prod − in-code-reader-cores is **non-reader producer-side PG CPU**, dominated by
  PG18 `io_method=worker` io-workers doing the heap reads (the reader's `ReadBufferExtended` delegates
  the physical read), plus backend deparse/dispatch and stream-worker lifecycle (spawn, columnizer init,
  EOS, socket-pump). It is consistent (~0.4–1.5 cores) and is *not* part of the read/deform/publish
  reader decomposition, by definition.
- G4 off_cons (/proc, whole-wall) ≥ query_log consumer cores (query-attributed) because the /proc window
  also captures CH background CPU (async metrics, housekeeping) during the run; the gap shrinks at high W
  / short wall where background is a smaller fraction.
